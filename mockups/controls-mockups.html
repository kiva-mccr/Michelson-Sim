Interferometer controls: three drag-first mockups

Each mockup is a 16:9 frame of the full page. The dashed pink outline marks what changes; grey boxes are the plots that stay as they are. Every control here really works: drag it and the sketch ring (a HeNe line, for speed) responds. Every number with a dashed underline can still be clicked and typed into. Double-click any tilt control to reset it to 0, 0.

A · The schematic is the control

The Interferometer panel widens into the ring's column and becomes a large top-down Michelson drawing. You grab the physical parts: slide M₂ along its rail for d, rotate either mirror by its end knob for tilt, and open or close the detector's lens cone for the field of view. Values ride beside each part. This one is the most literal and the best for teaching, but it takes the most room.

B · Rail, tilt pad and field-of-view arc

Three purpose-built instruments, each matched to its quantity. A long ruled rail for d (ticks rescale with the range, so it can show nanometres or tens of microns). A square pad shows the face of M₂: drag the dot to tilt in x and y at once, and the arrow shows the fringe direction. A protractor arc sets the field of view. It's compact, works in the current column width, and gives two-axis tilt in one gesture.

C · Drag directly on the outputs

No separate control widgets: you change the setup where you see its effect. Drag the ring's rim in or out for the field of view. Drag inside the ring to tilt; the fringes slide under your cursor and an arrow shows (αx, αy). Scrub the strip under the ring (like a jog wheel) for d; the further you drag, the faster it moves. The δ₀ marker on the interferogram is draggable too. This frees the most room (the ring gets the whole column) but hides the controls, so it relies on hints and the number strip.
