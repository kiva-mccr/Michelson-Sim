MS-7, MS-8, MS-9 and MS-11, each shown as it is now beside the proposal. The controls on this page work. I am building these proposals in this order unless you say otherwise; each step is its own PR and can be reverted.

MS-7: tilt on the fixed mirror M₁

Drag the dot on the pattern. Today the moving mirror M₂ turns by αx alone. In the proposal, M₂ only slides with d, and the fixed mirror M₁ turns by the total tilt √(αx² + αy²), exaggerated so you can see it (500 µrad draws as 10°). The pattern and its physics don't change: δ = 2d cosθ + 2(αx x + αy y) depends only on the tilt between the two mirror images, so the snapshot of every output stays identical apart from the schematic itself.

Now

Proposed · M₁ tilts by |α|

  • The label moves to M₁ and reads "M₁ tilt 134 µrad" (the magnitude); with no tilt it reads "M₁ fixed". The αx and αy chips under the pattern stay as they are and stay editable.
  • M₁ always turns the same way, since a magnitude has no sign. The azimuth stays visible on the ring, as the dashed cut line.

MS-9: glide when the tilt snaps to 0, 0

Drag each dot slowly towards the centre. Inside the dashed circle the tilt snaps to 0, 0. On the left it jumps in one frame, as now. On the right it glides there over 150 ms (ease-out), and glides back out to the cursor when you leave the circle. The trace below each pad is |α| over the last second.

Now · jumps

Proposed · 150 ms glide

  • The tilt value itself glides, not just the dot, so the pattern on the page shows the true pattern for every intermediate tilt (no faked frames). The frames during the glide are live previews; the exact full-resolution image follows when it lands.
  • Reset tilt and double-click glide the same way. With reduced motion turned on in the system settings, it jumps as now.

MS-11: preview resolution follows how fast the picture changes

Today the preview resolution is set only by what the device can draw in one display frame, and it stays coarse for as long as the mouse button is held, even when nothing moves. The proposal adds the pace of change: the budget for one preview is the time until the next change is expected (measured from the last few changes, at least one display frame, at most 100 ms), and as soon as the input stops changing the preview refines step by step to the exact full-resolution image, even with the button still down. Fast drags and sweeps keep the frame rate; slow drags and pauses get sharper. Illustration below, 60 frames (1 s) per row; the shades are made up to show the behaviour.
