MS-7: tilt on the fixed mirror M₁ (revision 2)

Drag the dot on the pattern. M₂ only slides with d. The fixed mirror M₁ shows both tilt components, exaggerated so you can see them (500 µrad draws as 10°). αx turns it in the plane of the drawing, either way. αy leans it towards or away from you: for +αy you see a shaded strip of its silvered face below the bar, and for −αy a strip of its dark back above it. The strip's depth follows |αy|. The physics doesn't change: δ = 2d cosθ + 2(αx x + αy y) depends only on the tilt between the two mirror images.

MS-9: glide when the tilt snaps to 0, 0

Drag each dot slowly towards the centre. Inside the dashed circle the tilt snaps to 0, 0. On the left it jumps in one frame, as now. On the right it glides there over 150 ms (ease-out), and glides back out to the cursor when you leave the circle. The trace below each pad is |α| over the last second.

MS-11: resolution follows the last frame's time, continuously (revision 2)

After every preview frame the page knows how long it took, t. Drawing cost scales with the number of pixels, so the scale that would just fit the budget B is s* = s·√(t / B) (a radial pattern costs ∝ 1/s, so there the square root is dropped). The next frame moves part of the way there in log space, s ← s·(s*/s)^0.5, aiming at 90% of the budget, so a noisy timing can't make it flicker. The preview is an exact footprint-averaged grid of any width from N/16 to N, scaled up; not just N/2, N/3, N/4. When nothing changes, it goes straight to the exact full-resolution image, even with the button still held.

Simulated: 4 s of dragging, budget 16.7 ms; the cost doubles at 1 s (tilt switched on) and the device slows at 2.5 s
