Michelson interferometer: from one line $\sigma_0$ to any spectrum $S(\sigma)$

Every result here comes from one building block. A single wavenumber leaves the interferometer as $\tfrac12\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]$ times its input. Each row adds one idea to the row before it. The last row is the general Fourier-transform result, and it contains all four rows above it.

Notation and the building block

$\sigma = 1/\lambda$ is wavenumber. $\delta$ is the optical path difference (OPD). Arms of lengths $L_1$ and $L_2$ give $\delta = 2(L_2-L_1)$ on axis. A ray at angle $\theta$ to the axis, with mirror separation $d$, sees $\delta = 2d\cos\theta$.[1]

The beamsplitter is a lossless 50:50 splitter and both arms reflect equally, so fringe visibility depends only on the spectrum. $\delta_D$ is the Dirac delta. $I_n$ is the power in line or band $n$, and $I_{\rm tot}=\sum_n I_n$. Gaussians use the $1/e$ half-width $w = \Delta\sigma/(2\sqrt{\ln 2})$, where $\Delta\sigma$ is the FWHM. For a narrow band, $\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta\lambda/\lambda_0^2$.

Each pass through the splitter multiplies the field by $1/\sqrt2$, so each arm returns with amplitude $\tfrac12$:

$$E_{\rm out}=\tfrac12E_0\left(e^{i4\pi\sigma L_1}+e^{i4\pi\sigma L_2}\right)\;\Rightarrow\;I_{\rm out}(\sigma,\delta)=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]$$

This is the textbook two-beam result.[2] Read it per wavenumber and it is a transfer function, $T(\sigma,\delta)=\tfrac12[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)]$.[3] All five columns below follow from it:

Interference pattern $I(\theta)$ = signal vs. ring angle at fixed $d$. Interferogram $I(\delta)$ = signal vs. mirror scan. Un-interfered spectrum $S(\sigma)$ = source. Interfered spectrum $S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=S(\sigma)\,T(\sigma,\delta)$. Coherence length $l_c$ = OPD over which the fringes wash out.

Coherence-length definitions differ by a constant. Row 3 gives the half width at half maximum (HWHM) of $|\gamma|$. Rows 4–5 use Mandel's $\int|\gamma|^2\,d\delta$.[11] For a Gaussian, Mandel's definition gives $\sqrt{2\ln2/\pi}\,\lambda_0^2/\Delta\lambda\approx0.664\,\lambda_0^2/\Delta\lambda$, compared with $0.441\,\lambda_0^2/\Delta\lambda$ for the HWHM. Pick one definition and use it throughout.

Row 1

Single wavelength $\lambda_0 = 1/\sigma_0$

Starting point: the building block applied to a spectrum that contains only $\sigma_0$.

Un-interfered spectrum

$$S(\sigma)=I_0\,\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_0)$$

Interferogram

$$I(\delta)=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi\delta}{\lambda_0}\right)\right]$$

This is the Le Mans two-beam result.[2] One fringe passes for every $\lambda_0/2$ of mirror travel.

Interference pattern (rings)

$$I(\theta)=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+\cos\!\left(\frac{4\pi d\cos\theta}{\lambda_0}\right)\right],\qquad \text{bright rings at } 2d\cos\theta_m=m\lambda_0$$

The geometry follows from the two virtual sources $2d$ apart.[1] Some treatments add a $\pi$ shift for the external reflection, which swaps bright and dark rings.

Interfered spectrum

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=S(\sigma)\,\tfrac12\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma_0\delta)\right]\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_0)$$

The per-wavenumber transmission is $A(\delta,\sigma)\propto 1+\cos(2\pi\delta\sigma)$.[3] Integrating the interfered spectrum over $\sigma$ gives back the interferogram, $\int S_{\rm out}\,d\sigma = I(\delta)$. That identity links columns 2 and 4 in every row.

Coherence length

$$\gamma(\delta)=e^{i2\pi\sigma_0\delta},\qquad V(\delta)=|\gamma(\delta)|=1\ \ \forall\,\delta\;\Rightarrow\; l_c=\infty$$

An ideal monochromatic line never loses contrast. A real laser has a finite linewidth $\Delta\nu$, so $l_c\sim c/\Delta\nu$. Row 3 makes that precise.

Row 2

Sum of $N$ wavelengths $\{\sigma_n, I_n\}$

Builds on Row 1: lines at different frequencies have no stationary cross term, so their intensities add.[5] Each line contributes its own Row 1 interferogram. This row also introduces $\gamma(\delta)$, the complex degree of coherence.

Un-interfered spectrum

$$S(\sigma)=\sum_{n=1}^{N} I_n\,\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_n)$$

Interferogram

$$I(\delta)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{I_n}{2}\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma_n\delta)\right]=\frac{I_{\rm tot}}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{Re}\gamma(\delta)\Big],\qquad \gamma(\delta)=\frac{1}{I_{\rm tot}}\sum_n I_n e^{i2\pi\sigma_n\delta}$$

The first form is the sum of single-line intensities.[5] The $\gamma$ form is a rewrite derived.

Interference pattern

$$I(\theta)=\sum_n\frac{I_n}{2}\left[1+\cos(4\pi\sigma_n d\cos\theta)\right]$$

Row 1's ring formula, summed over the lines derived.

Interfered spectrum

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=\sum_n\frac{I_n}{2}\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma_n\delta)\right]\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_n)$$

Each line is scaled by the same transfer function $T(\sigma_n,\delta)$.[3]

Visibility and coherence length

$$V(\delta)=|\gamma(\delta)|=\frac{\left|\sum_n I_n e^{i2\pi\sigma_n\delta}\right|}{\sum_n I_n}$$

Two lines $\sigma_{1,2}$ with $\Delta\sigma=|\sigma_1-\sigma_2|$:

$$V=\frac{\sqrt{I_1^2+I_2^2+2I_1I_2\cos(2\pi\Delta\sigma\,\delta)}}{I_1+I_2}\;\xrightarrow{I_1=I_2}\;\left|\cos(\pi\Delta\sigma\,\delta)\right|$$

The fringes vanish first at $\delta=\lambda^2/(2\Delta\lambda)$ and revive with beat period $\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda$. This is the sodium-doublet beat experiment.[6] For Na D, $\lambda=589.3$ nm and $\Delta\lambda=0.597$ nm, so the beat period is $\approx0.58$ mm of OPD, or $0.29$ mm of mirror travel.

$N$ equal lines, evenly spaced by $\Delta\sigma_s$ (a geometric series derived):

$$V(\delta)=\left|\frac{\sin(N\pi\Delta\sigma_s\delta)}{N\sin(\pi\Delta\sigma_s\delta)}\right|,\qquad l_c\approx\frac{1}{N\Delta\sigma_s}=\frac{\lambda^2}{N\Delta\lambda_s},\quad\text{revivals every }\frac{\lambda^2}{\Delta\lambda_s}$$

Discrete lines never lose coherence for good. $|\gamma|$ keeps reviving, so Mandel's integral $\int|\gamma|^2d\delta$ diverges, as it does in Row 1. That is why this row uses the first null as its coherence length.

Row 3

Gaussian spectrum $S(\sigma)\propto e^{-(\sigma-\sigma_0)^2/w^2}$

Builds on Row 2: let the lines merge into a continuum, $\sum_n I_n\,\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_n)\to S(\sigma)$, so the sum becomes an integral. The revivals from Row 2 disappear, and $|\gamma|$ now decays for good.

Un-interfered spectrum

$$S(\sigma)=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt\pi\,w}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(\sigma-\sigma_0)^2}{w^2}\right],\qquad w=\frac{\Delta\sigma}{2\sqrt{\ln2}},\quad\int S\,d\sigma=I_0$$

The same normalized form appears as $S(k)=\frac{1}{\Delta k\sqrt\pi}e^{-[(k-k_0)/\Delta k]^2}$.[7]

Interferogram

$$\gamma(\delta)=\frac{1}{I_0}\int S(\sigma)\,e^{i2\pi\sigma\delta}d\sigma=e^{-(\pi w\delta)^2}\,e^{i2\pi\sigma_0\delta}$$
$$I(\delta)=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+e^{-(\pi w\delta)^2}\cos(2\pi\sigma_0\delta)\right]=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2\Delta\sigma^2\delta^2}{4\ln2}\right)\cos(2\pi\sigma_0\delta)\right]$$

The Fourier transform of a Gaussian is a Gaussian.[7] The cosine-transform relation between spectrum and interferogram is the Toronto FTS result.[4]

Interference pattern

$$I(\theta)=\frac{I_0}{2}\left[1+e^{-(2\pi w d\cos\theta)^2}\cos(4\pi\sigma_0 d\cos\theta)\right]$$

The interferogram with $\delta\to2d\cos\theta$ derived. Rings are visible only while $2d\lesssim l_c$.

Interfered spectrum

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=\frac{I_0}{2\sqrt\pi\,w}\,e^{-(\sigma-\sigma_0)^2/w^2}\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]$$

A Gaussian with channel fringes across it, spaced $1/\delta$ apart in $\sigma$.[3]

Coherence length

$$l_c=\frac{2\ln2}{\pi}\,\frac{\lambda_0^2}{\Delta\lambda}\approx0.441\,\frac{\lambda_0^2}{\Delta\lambda}\quad(\text{HWHM of }|\gamma|)$$

Laser Focus World gives this formula and defines it as the HWHM of the coherence function.[8] Example: an 840 nm source with 50 nm FWHM gives $l_c\approx6.2\ \mu$m. Mandel's definition gives $\int|\gamma|^2d\delta=1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,w)\approx0.664\,\lambda_0^2/\Delta\lambda$ derived.

Row 4

Sum of $N$ Gaussians $\sum_n G_n(\sigma;\sigma_n,w_n)$

Builds on Rows 2 and 3: use Row 2's sum, but make each term one of Row 3's Gaussians. Each band brings its own Gaussian envelope and carrier. The beats between bands come back as side lobes.

Un-interfered spectrum

$$S(\sigma)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{I_n}{\sqrt\pi\,w_n}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(\sigma-\sigma_n)^2}{w_n^2}\right]$$

Interferogram

$$I(\delta)=\frac{I_{\rm tot}}{2}+\frac12\sum_n I_n\,e^{-(\pi w_n\delta)^2}\cos(2\pi\sigma_n\delta),\qquad \gamma(\delta)=\frac{1}{I_{\rm tot}}\sum_n I_n\,e^{-(\pi w_n\delta)^2}e^{i2\pi\sigma_n\delta}$$

US 7,732,784, Eq. 33, writes the combined-source signal as a superposition of Gaussian envelopes times cosine carriers, one term per source.[9]

Interference pattern

$$I(\theta)=\frac{I_{\rm tot}}{2}+\frac12\sum_n I_n\,e^{-(2\pi w_n d\cos\theta)^2}\cos(4\pi\sigma_n d\cos\theta)$$

derived by substituting $\delta=2d\cos\theta$.

Interfered spectrum

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=\tfrac12\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]\sum_n\frac{I_n}{\sqrt\pi\,w_n}e^{-(\sigma-\sigma_n)^2/w_n^2}$$

Visibility and coherence length

Two equal bands of equal width $w$:

$$|\gamma(\delta)|=e^{-(\pi w\delta)^2}\left|\cos(\pi\Delta\sigma_{12}\delta)\right|$$

This is Row 2's beat multiplied by Row 3's envelope. Jansz et al. found that multi-Gaussian sources raise the side lobes in the coherence function, while a pure Gaussian has none.[10] In general the HWHM has no closed form; solve $|\gamma(\delta)|=\tfrac12$ numerically. Mandel's integral does have a closed form:

$$l_c=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma|^2d\delta=\frac{1}{I_{\rm tot}^2}\sum_{m,n}\frac{I_mI_n}{\sqrt{\pi\,(w_m^2+w_n^2)}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(\sigma_m-\sigma_n)^2}{w_m^2+w_n^2}\right]$$

derived from Mandel's definition[11] using $\int e^{-a\delta^2}\cos(b\delta)\,d\delta=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-b^2/4a}$. With $N=1$ it reduces to Row 3's $1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,w)$. Each single band also has its own Row 3 length, $l_{c,n}=(2\ln2/\pi)\,\lambda_n^2/\Delta\lambda_n$.

Row 5

Arbitrary spectrum $S(\sigma)$: the Wiener–Khinchin form

Builds on everything above: drop every assumption about the shape of $S(\sigma)$. Rows 1–4 are this row with a specific $S$ plugged in.

Interferogram and its inverse

$$I(\delta)=\frac12\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right]d\sigma=\frac{I_{\rm tot}}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{Re}\gamma(\delta)\Big]$$
$$I_R(\delta)=\int_0^\infty B(\sigma)\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\,d\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B(\sigma)=\int_0^\infty I_R(\delta)\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\,d\delta$$

The Toronto FTS lab (Eqs. 15–16) shows that, up to a constant, the spectrum is the cosine Fourier transform of the interferogram.[4] Here $I_R$ is the oscillating part $I(\delta)-I_{\rm tot}/2$.

Complex degree of coherence (Wiener–Khinchin)

$$\gamma(\delta)=\frac{\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\,e^{i2\pi\sigma\delta}\,d\sigma}{\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\,d\sigma},\qquad V(\delta)=|\gamma(\delta)|$$

The Michelson measures the field autocorrelation, and the Wiener–Khinchin theorem turns that into the power spectrum.[12]

Interference pattern

$$I(\theta)=\frac12\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\left[1+\cos(4\pi\sigma d\cos\theta)\right]d\sigma$$

Interfered spectrum

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta)=S(\sigma)\,\tfrac12\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\right],\qquad \int_0^\infty S_{\rm out}\,d\sigma=I(\delta)$$

The output spectrum is channeled, and the fringe density grows with the OPD. Spectral-domain OCT reads $\delta$ from this.[3]

Coherence length

$$l_c=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(\delta)|^2\,d\delta=\frac{\int_0^\infty S^2(\sigma)\,d\sigma}{\left(\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\,d\sigma\right)^2}$$

The left side is Mandel's coherence time, $\tau_c=\int|\gamma(t)|^2dt$, times $c$.[11] The right side follows from Parseval's theorem derived. A broader or flatter spectrum gives a shorter $l_c$.
