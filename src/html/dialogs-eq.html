Source spectrum $S(\sigma)$: equations and notes

$$S(\sigma)=\sum_{\rm lines}I_n\,\delta_D(\sigma-\sigma_n)+\sum_{\rm Gauss}a_n\,e^{-(\sigma-\sigma_n)^2/w_n^2}$$
$$I_n=\int S_n\,d\sigma=\begin{cases}\sqrt\pi\,a_n w_n&\text{Gaussian}\\ \sqrt\pi\,a_n w_0&\text{line}\end{cases}$$
$$w_0=\frac{0.008\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}}{2\sqrt{\ln2}}$$
$$w_n=\frac{\Delta\sigma_n}{2\sqrt{\ln2}}$$
$$\Delta\sigma_n\approx\frac{\Delta\lambda_n}{\lambda_n^2},\qquad \sigma=\frac{1}{\lambda}$$

Components add as incoherent sums. Each Gaussian's height $a_n$ is its peak spectral density, so its power $I_n$ is the area under the curve you see. A line has zero width, so its height stands for the power it would have as the narrowest allowed Gaussian (FWHM $0.008\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, where dragging snaps back to a line); its power therefore doesn't jump when you widen it.[5][7]

How to edit

Click empty space to add a wavelength. Drag a dot to move it or change its power. To widen it into a Gaussian, drag its vertical line or either end of its width bar sideways. Drag back to the centre to make it a line again. Double-click a dot, or select it and press Delete, to remove it. Untick a component to switch it off without deleting it.

Units and display

$\sigma$ is in µm⁻¹, and $\delta$ and $d$ are in µm. Every curve is drawn at its true height with no rescaling: a component set to 1 peaks at 1 in both the source and the interfered spectrum. The vertical axis is spectral density per unit $\sigma$. The dashed curve is the sum of all the Gaussians, and the axis extends above 1 only when overlapping Gaussians add up past it. Click any number with a dashed underline to type an exact value. Your setup is saved in this browser.

Interferometer: equations and notes

$$I(x,y)=\tfrac12\int_0^\infty S(\sigma)\left[1+\cos 2\pi\sigma\,\delta(x,y)\right]d\sigma$$
$$\delta(x,y)=2d\cos\theta+2(\alpha_x x+\alpha_y y)$$
$$\tan\theta=\frac{r}{r_{\max}}\tan\theta_{\max}$$

$x,y$ are mirror-plane coordinates in mm. The visible half-width of the mirror is $a=L\tan\theta_{\max}$ with $L=28.4$ mm (5 mm at 10°), so the field of view zooms the rings and the tilt wedge together.[1][4][14] The tilt $(\alpha_x,\alpha_y)$ is on the fixed mirror $M_1$ and $M_2$ only moves by $d$. Only the tilt between the two mirror images enters $\delta$, so it would be the same on either mirror. The schematic shows the tilt on $M_1$, greatly exaggerated: $\alpha_x$ turns it in the plane of the drawing, and $\alpha_y$ leans it towards you (its silvered face shows, for $\alpha_y>0$) or away (its back shows, for $\alpha_y<0$).

  • Colours are what an eye would see, from the CVRL CIE 2006 colour-matching functions.[13]
  • Light outside 390–780 nm adds no colour, but it still counts in the brightness profile. Its markers in the source spectrum are drawn grey so they stay visible.
  • Each pixel is the exact average of the pattern over its footprint, so rings finer than a pixel blend smoothly instead of forming false moiré.
  • The pattern is drawn by the fastest renderer on your device. The page starts with the first one available in the order WebGPU, WebGL2, the CPU on all cores (Web Workers), the CPU on one core. Once the first image is done, a short speed test has each of them compute the same image, then switches to the fastest and remembers it for this device. The ? in the corner of this panel shows which one is in use, the test's times, and the resolution it is drawing at. All four evaluate the same per-pixel formula. On the GPU, the large part of each fringe phase, $k_b\cdot 2d \bmod 2\pi$, is computed in 64-bit on the CPU, and the cosine is taken with an accurate series, because GPU arithmetic is 32-bit. The "Ring pixels" check reads the active renderer's pixels back and compares them with brute-force integration.
  • The profile below is a cut through the centre along the dashed line: the tilt direction $(\alpha_x,\alpha_y)$, or $x$ with no tilt.
  • The profile and the interferogram are anti-aliased range plots: each pixel is shaded by how much of it the true signal covers, using 8 sub-columns per pixel. Fringes near the pixel pitch blend into an even tone instead of forming moiré, and where they are finer than a pixel the fill spans the full swing $\tfrac12(1\pm|\gamma|)$.

Interferogram $I(\delta)$: equations and notes

$$I(\delta)=\frac{I_{\rm tot}}{2}+\frac12\sum_n I_n\,e^{-(\pi w_n\delta)^2}\cos(2\pi\sigma_n\delta)$$
$$w_n=\frac{\Delta\sigma_n}{2\sqrt{\ln2}}$$

$w_n=0$ for a line.[4][7][9]

Interfered spectrum $S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta_0)$: equations and notes

$$S_{\rm out}(\sigma;\delta_0)=S(\sigma)\cdot\tfrac12\left[1+\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta_0)\right]$$
$$\delta_0=2d$$

The channel spacing is $\Delta\sigma=1/\delta_0$.[3]

The dashed outline is the true shape of $S(\sigma)$ and the thin grey curve is $T(\sigma,\delta_0)$.

Coherence length $l_c$: equations and notes

$$|\gamma(l_c^{\rm HWHM})|=\tfrac12$$
$$l_c^{\rm Mandel}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(\delta)|^2\,d\delta$$
$$l_{c,n}=\frac{2\ln2}{\pi}\frac{\lambda_n^2}{\Delta\lambda_n}$$
$$\gamma(\delta)=\frac{1}{I_{\rm tot}}\sum_n I_n\,e^{-(\pi w_n\delta)^2}e^{i2\pi\sigma_n\delta}$$

[6][8][10][11][12]

With your current settings:

HWHM is the first OPD where $|\gamma(\delta)|$ falls to ½, found numerically up to 5 mm. Mandel's definition is $l_c=\int|\gamma|^2d\delta$, which has a closed form for lines plus Gaussians and is infinite if any pure line is present. A single Gaussian's own length is $\tfrac{2\ln2}{\pi}\lambda_n^2/\Delta\lambda_n$.
