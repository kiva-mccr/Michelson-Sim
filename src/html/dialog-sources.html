Sources

  1. University of Toronto, PHY224/324 lab notes, "Interferometers": the two virtual sources sit $2d$ apart, so the path difference is $2d\cos\theta$. Also J. C. Wyant, Optics 505, Session 4 (Haidinger fringes, $2d\cos\theta=m\lambda$).
  2. Le Mans Université, optics course exercise: $I=\tfrac{I_0}{2}\left(1+\cos\frac{2\pi\delta}{\lambda}\right)$.
  3. arXiv:2410.21586, "Multiple-beam Interference Spectroscopy", Eq. 2: $A(\delta,\sigma)=2\mathcal T(\sigma)\left(1+\cos(2\pi\delta\sigma)\right)$ for the Michelson (different normalization). Channeled output spectrum: US 11,690,516.
  4. University of Toronto, "Fourier Transform Spectroscopy" lab, Eqs. 15–16: $I_R(\delta)=\int B(\sigma)\cos(2\pi\sigma\delta)\,d\sigma$ and its inverse.
  5. Michelson interferometer lab report (SlideShare, 2015): dichromatic intensity as the sum of the two single-wavelength terms. Additive multi-wavelength interferograms: US 12,174,070.
  6. University of Maryland, Phys 375 Lab 4 (two-frequency beat signal); Michigan State, PHY 431 Lab 5 (sodium beat pattern).
  7. arXiv:2002.08755, swept-source OCT dissertation, Eqs. 1.9–1.10: Gaussian $S(k)$ and $\gamma(z)=e^{-z^2\Delta k^2}$.
  8. M. Duelk, Laser Focus World: $l_c=\frac{2\ln2}{\pi}\frac{\lambda_0^2}{\delta\lambda}$, defined as the HWHM. Also J. Biomed. Opt. 12, 014017 (2007).
  9. US 7,732,784, Eq. 33: the combined-source OCT signal as $\sum_i I_i\,e^{-(\cdot)t^2}\cos(\cdot)$.
  10. P. Jansz et al., "Modeling of low coherence interferometry using broadband multi-Gaussian light sources", Photonic Sensors 2, 247 (2012).
  11. arXiv:1210.5944, Appendix B: $\tau_c=\int|\gamma(t)|^2dt$ (Mandel's definition).
  12. University of Toronto, ESC471 FTS manual (Wiener–Khinchin with a Michelson); Peking University, coherence lecture notes.
  13. CVRL (Colour & Vision Research Laboratory, UCL), CIE 2006 “physiologically-relevant” 2° XYZ colour-matching functions (CIE 170-2:2015), used for every colour on the page. Background: A. Stockman, “Cone fundamentals and CIE standards”. XYZ is converted to sRGB with the IEC 61966-2-1 matrix, and out-of-gamut colours are desaturated toward white.
  14. Tilted mirror: a tilt about one axis adds an optical path that changes linearly across the mirror, from “Quantitative phase determination by using a Michelson interferometer”, Eur. J. Phys. The air film becomes a wedge, and the fringes go from circular to curved to straight: NISER Michelson lab manual.

Textbook references for the same results: E. Hecht, Optics, §9.4 and Ch. 12; L. Mandel & E. Wolf, Optical Coherence and Quantum Optics, §4.3; R. J. Bell, Introductory Fourier Transform Spectroscopy. Formulas marked “derived” in the companion reference page follow from these by substitution; the simulator evaluates them directly.
